AI acqui-hiring spree continues with Anthropic snapping up Humanloop team

by Ann O’Dea

14 Aug 2025

From left: Peter Hayes, Jordan Burgess and Raza Habib. Image: calvinaphotography.com

The part Irish-founded UK AI start-up Humanloop has wound down with its three co-founders and most of its engineering team joining Anthropic.

Having signalled it was shutting down ahead of an acquisition last month, it has now been confirmed that Humanloop’s three co-founders – CEO Raza Habib, CTO Irishman Peter Hayes and CPO Jordan Burgess – have all gone to Anthropic, as well as most of its engineering and research team.

An Anthropic spokesperson confirmed to TechCrunch that it had not acquired Humanloop’s assets or its intellectual property, a pretty moot point given that most of the IP is likely to live in the heads of its founders and key staff.

It appears to be part of a new trend in the AI talent war, with recent examples of such acqui-hire deals as Google swooping in to bring Windsurf CEO Varun Mohan, co-founder Douglas Chen and other members of the coding tool’s R&D team to Google DeepMind. OpenAI had been attempting to finalise a deal with Windsurf at the time.

Founded in University College London, the AI enterprise tooling spin-out later participated in Y Combinator (YC) and the Fuse Incubator, and raised nearly $8m seed funding in rounds led by YC and Index Ventures. Humanloop has worked with enterprise clients from Duolingo to Vanta and specialises in LLM evaluation and observability.

In a statement on their website attributed to the Humanloop founders and “the whole humanloop team”, they said they were announcing “the Humanloop team is joining Anthropic”.

“From the start, our mission at Humanloop has been to enable the safe and rapid adoption of AI,” it read. “Now, as the pace of AI progress accelerates, we think Anthropic is the ideal home to amplify our impact.

“We are grateful to our investors: Albion, Index, YC, Local Globe, UCLTF and many fantastic angels,” the statement continued, although it seems likely these investors have been burnt in the deal – no financial details were disclosed. “Building this company has been an incredible journey and we were fortunate to have investors who trusted and supported us at every step.”

They went on to say that they were very proud of what they had built at Humanloop. “We were the first development platform for LLM applications and we shaped industry standards for how to manage and evaluate AI,” they said. “We’re excited about this next chapter, joining Anthropic to help build an AI future that benefits everyone.”

Anthropic was founded by siblings and Open AI alumni Dario and Daniela Amodei. Still a private company, it was built on the principles of responsible AI, although many commentators have questioned the ability of any AI player to be truly ethical. In recent years it has received funding from the likes of Amazon and Google, drawing ire from online purists.

In July it was reported that Anthropic was raising as much as $5bn in a round led by global investment firm Iconiq Capital, a deal that would value the start-up at $170bn, seeing it catching up with the likes of OpenAI, which is valued at $300bn, the Chinese TikTok-owner ByteDance at $312bn and SpaceX, which stands at about $400bn.

Anthropic announced plans to create more than 100 new jobs across its European offices in May, with a primary focus on hiring in Dublin and London. The company opened its first EU office last year in Dublin, to support its European expansion.

The start-up released its Claude 4 models in May, claiming that Claude Opus 4 was the “best coding model in the world”.
